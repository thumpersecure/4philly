What is a Right-to-Know request?

Pennsylvania's Right-to-Know Law (65 P.S. § 67.101 et seq.) lets any person ask a government agency for its public records. For a Philadelphia building, that agency is the Department of Licenses & Inspections (L&I).

  • What you can ask for: inspection reports, inspectors' notes and photos, letters and emails about the property, and complaints on file. Ask for records, not answers to questions. The agency may withhold records the law exempts.
  • Free to file. You pay nothing to ask. The agency may charge for copies, and you set a limit on the form, asking them to check with you first.
  • The clock: the agency must answer within 5 business days (it can take a 30-day extension). No answer by the deadline counts as a denial. If you're denied, you have 15 business days to appeal to the state Office of Open Records.
  • Who can file: anyone who is a legal resident of the United States, using their real name and contact details. The form asks you to confirm this.

Source: the Pennsylvania Office of Open Records' Standard Right-to-Know Law Request Form (the same form this page fills in) and openrecords.pa.gov. This is general information, not legal advice.
