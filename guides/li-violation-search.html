Philadelphia L&I Code Violation Search: Check Any Address Free

Every violation Licenses & Inspections has ever logged against a Philadelphia property is public record. Here's how to pull it, read it, and verify it.

Published August 1, 2026

4PHILLY is an independent, non-governmental tool — not operated, affiliated with, or endorsed by the City of Philadelphia. This guide is legal information, not legal advice. For advice about your specific situation, contact a housing attorney or Community Legal Services (215-981-3700). Verify any record directly at atlas.phila.gov.

What an L&I violation is

When a Philadelphia Licenses & Inspections inspector finds a property out of compliance with the City's building, fire, or property maintenance codes, they issue a violation — a dated, numbered citation naming the code section and what was found: no heat, unsafe wiring, structural problems, pest infestation, missing smoke detectors, and so on. Each violation carries a case number and a status, and the whole history stays on the property's public record.

For a renter, that history answers a question no listing photo can: what has the City already caught at this address, and did the owner fix it?

How to run the search

  1. Go to 4philly.net and type the property's address. Free, no account, nothing stored.
  2. Or search by OPA number. Every Philadelphia parcel has a unique OPA account number. One street address can cover multiple parcels — a corner building, a subdivided lot — so the address alone can be ambiguous. The OPA number never is. 4PHILLY resolves your address to its OPA number and keys every record to it.
  3. Read the violations panel. You'll see each violation's case number, date, code section, title, and status — alongside the same parcel's rental license status, permits, building certifications, and 311 complaints.

Reading the results: OPEN vs COMPLIED

StatusWhat it means
OPENUnresolved as recorded. The City cited the condition and has not recorded compliance.
COMPLIEDCompliance was recorded in the system. Important caveat: that is not the same as an inspector physically re-visiting — a violation can be closed administratively. COMPLIED means "currently uncontested in the record," not "verified fixed."

A pattern is more informative than any single entry. A building with a long history of violations that each took months to close tells you something the current snapshot doesn't.

The most serious finding: Unfit Structure

An Unfit Structure designation (code PM15-109.1) means an inspector determined the building is unfit for human habitation or use — the most serious finding L&I can issue for a residential property. Common triggers: structural failure, no running water or working sanitation, no heat in winter, severe fire-safety deficiencies, hazardous electrical conditions. If an address shows an open Unfit Structure citation, a current tenant should contact Community Legal Services immediately (215-981-3700) and may have grounds to break the lease without penalty — that's a question for a lawyer, not a website.

How fresh is the data?

Philadelphia publishes violations through two pipelines, and they don't move at the same speed:

  • Eclipse — L&I's live system, the authoritative record. 4PHILLY's property lookup queries it directly, so what you see reflects the current state.
  • Carto — the City's open-data mirror, used for area-wide scans. It refreshes daily but can lag the live record by days to weeks on individual changes.

Practical rule: use scans to find candidates, then confirm any specific address with a direct lookup — and for anything you'd put in front of a landlord, lawyer, or judge, verify against the City's own systems.

Verifying and going deeper

  • Atlas — the City's official property lookup. Confirm the parcel boundaries and the OPA number, then review the same violation history from the source.
  • L&I eCLIPSE — the authoritative record for licenses, violations, permits, and certifications. This is the record that holds up for a lease decision or a legal filing.
  • Inspector notes and site photos are never public on their own. To get them, file a Right-to-Know Law (RTKL) request with L&I's Open Records Officer. The 4PHILLY Right-to-Know page fills in the official state form for you — address, OPA number, and case numbers pulled straight from the record you're viewing. Screenshots aren't certified evidence; RTKL copies from the City are.

What the violation record can't tell you

Public data only contains what was reported and inspected. It won't show unreported problems, the inspector's identity or notes, who filed a 311 complaint (that's anonymized by design, and retaliation for reporting is illegal under PA Landlord-Tenant Act § 5720), or whether a re-inspection physically occurred. A clean record is a good sign, not a guarantee — and a bad record is a documented starting point, not a verdict on any person.
