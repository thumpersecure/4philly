How to Check a Landlord's Rental License in Philadelphia (Free)

Every Philadelphia rental needs an active license. Checking one takes under a minute — and the answer changes what your landlord can legally collect.

Published August 1, 2026

4PHILLY is an independent, non-governmental tool — not operated, affiliated with, or endorsed by the City of Philadelphia. This guide is legal information, not legal advice. For advice about your specific situation, contact a housing attorney or Community Legal Services (215-981-3700). Verify any record directly at atlas.phila.gov.

Why the rental license matters

Philadelphia doesn't treat the rental license as paperwork trivia. Under Phila. Code § 9-3902(1)(a), a landlord may not collect rent on a property that requires a rental license unless a valid license is currently in force. Under § 9-3901(4)(e), an owner who lets the license lapse cannot collect rent or evict for nonpayment during the period of non-compliance.

In other words: an expired or missing license isn't a clerical detail. It goes directly to whether the person asking for your rent check is legally entitled to it. Pennsylvania's Superior Court confirmed the teeth in Frempong v. Richardson, 209 A.3d 1001 (Pa. Super. 2019) — tenants may recover rent paid during unlicensed periods.

How to check a license in under a minute

  1. Go to 4philly.net — free, no account, nothing stored.
  2. Type the property's address (or its OPA account number, if you have it — that's Philadelphia's unique parcel ID, and it's unambiguous where a street address can span several parcels).
  3. Read the rental license panel. You'll see the current status, the license number, the expiration date, and the number of units it covers — pulled live from the City's own Eclipse (L&I) system.
  4. Verify. For anything you plan to act on, confirm the record at atlas.phila.gov or through L&I's eCLIPSE portal. Those are the authoritative government records.

You can do the whole check without 4PHILLY, too — Atlas and eCLIPSE are public. What 4PHILLY adds is the join: license status, L&I violations, permits, and 311 complaints for the same parcel on one page, keyed by the OPA number.

What each status actually means

StatusWhat it means
ActiveA valid rental license is currently in force. This is what you want to see.
ExpiredThe expiration date has passed and no renewal is in force. Under § 9-3902(1)(a), the authority to collect rent ends at expiration — not at some later bookkeeping date.
No license foundNo rental license is on record for the parcel. If the property is rented as housing, that's the same legal problem as an expired license — § 9-3902 requires one.

The 60-day blind spot on the City's own map

Every rental license carries two dates, and only one of them has legal weight. The expiration date is when the authority to collect rent ends. About 60 days later, the license is reclassified "Inactive" — an internal bookkeeping change, not a legal deadline.

The City's public map tools publish the second date, not the first. For the full window between them, a lapsed license shows no expiration date and a status that hasn't yet flipped — so the property looks unremarkable on the map even though, right now, it isn't legally licensed to collect rent. 4PHILLY's License Gap Scanner exists specifically to surface properties sitting in that window.

What to do if the license is expired or missing

  • Don't stop paying rent on your own. Improper withholding can get you evicted even when the landlord is in the wrong. Read your rights when a landlord is unlicensed for what the law actually provides, including the court-supervised escrow process.
  • Ask the landlord for the license number in writing. Then check it against the public record yourself.
  • Report it. File at phila.gov/311 (or dial 3-1-1). That creates a public case number tied to the address, and retaliation for filing is illegal under the PA Landlord-Tenant Act § 5720.
  • Talk to legal aid. Community Legal Services (215-981-3700) or Philadelphia Legal Assistance (215-981-3800) — free for eligible Philadelphians, and housing is a core practice area for both.

Common questions

Does an expired license mean I can stop paying rent?

No — never unilaterally. The landlord's right to collect is impaired under § 9-3902, but the safe path for you runs through a housing attorney or the court-supervised rent escrow process. Contact Community Legal Services before withholding anything.

The landlord says the license is "being renewed." Does that matter?

What matters legally is whether a valid license is currently in force. Ask for the license number and verify the status yourself — the record is public, and checking takes a minute.

How current is the data?

4PHILLY's property lookup reads the City's Eclipse system live; its scanners read the City's daily-refreshed open data. The City's map tools, as explained above, can run up to 60 days behind on lapsed licenses. For anything consequential, verify at atlas.phila.gov as of today's date.

Can the landlord see that I checked?

No. These are public records. There's no account, no login, and no notification to anyone — 4PHILLY doesn't keep a record of who looked up what.
